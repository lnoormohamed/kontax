Principles

  • Type does the work: 80px display, tight tracking, mono index labels.
  • Green is for brand and action; stone is for surfaces.
  • Asymmetric 5/7 section heads instead of centred stacks.

Keeps · changes

  • Keeps: palette core, dark-green footer, window mockups, whitespace.
  • Changes: real typeface, green family, stone neutral, hairline wires, left-aligned grid.

Risks

  • Still the least distinctive. With the logo covered it reads as “good calm SaaS”.
  • The stone band and dark footer need a hairline between them. Handled here with a border.

Signature: three into one

The same person saved in Google, iCloud and Fastmail is wired into one Kontax contact. Each field is tagged with its source and the result syncs back to iPhone, Mac and Google. On load: the sources slide in, the wires draw, the merged card rises and its fields tick in (1.8 s, transform and opacity only, runs once).

Reduced motion and no JS: the markup is the final frame, so nothing is hidden before the animation. The .pre start state is only added by JS. On mobile, one source card and a caption stand in for all three.

Direction BPersonal & warm

Built around “your address book, yours to keep”. Paper and ink replace white and green-on-white. Newsreader, a text serif, sets the headlines. Terracotta and ochre join the green. The motif is the paper address book itself: index cards, A–Z thumb tabs and stamped facts.

Principles

  • Human, not software: serif voice, paper surfaces, one italic accent per headline.
  • The product window is an index card, so product and metaphor are the same object.
  • Decoration is structural (tabs, rules, stamps), never figurative drawing.

Keeps · changes

  • Keeps: forest green for actions and the footer, the logo, live mockups, the calm copy.
  • Changes: paper palette, serif headlines, terracotta/ochre accents, card-file hero, new headline “Your address book, yours to keep.”

Risks

  • Can tip into twee. Guardrail: at most one italic word per headline and no illustrated people.
  • A serif can read as less “technical” to teams and switchers. Mitigated by the product UI and the comparison table.
  • Terracotta passes AA as text only at #a94a24; the lighter tint is decoration only.

Signature: the card file

A fanned stack of index cards filed under R, with an A–Z thumb-index rail. On load, the stack fans out from a neat pile, the front card (Rosa Martínez, Family book, on 4 phones) drops in, and the R tab slides out (1.3 s, transform and opacity only).

Reduced motion and no JS: the fanned stack is the resting HTML. The whole figure has one text alternative. The A–Z rail carries into /help as a real jump-to-letter control.

MEffort · app & auth

New tokens, a second font family (Newsreader variable, Latin subset, about 40 KB per style), the index-card frame, the tab label, the stamp. CSS transitions only.

App: the serif stays on marketing only; the app keeps Geist but can borrow the paper tints for empty states and onboarding. Auth: paper background, serif headline, green buttons. Medium, because it has to feel like the same product.

The hero is the product: a working search with keyboard shortcuts and a sync log. Light throughout; even the footer goes light.

Principles

  • Show, don’t describe: the hero can be used.
  • Precision from grid, mono and contrast. No dark bands.
  • Colour means state: blue is interactive or “us”, green is synced.

Keeps · changes

  • Keeps: logo tile colour, honest copy, live mockups.
  • Changes: ink buttons, blue accent, Plex type, blueprint grid, mono spec list in the hero, light footer (removes the dark footer/CTA patch from P49), new headline “One address book. Every account. In step.”

Risks

  • Can feel cold to families, the Family plan’s audience.
  • Leaning on the look of developer tools. Kept original by using contacts data and sync logs, not terminal clichés.
  • The interactive hero needs care: listbox semantics, focus, and a server-rendered list.

Signature: the live console

A real search over sample contacts (type, ↑/↓, / to focus). On load it types “ben”, selects Ben Nakamura, and the sync log shows one phone-number edit being saved and pushed to Google, iCloud and a Mac. Typing yourself stops the autoplay at once.

Reduced motion: no typing and a full log; the demo stays fully usable. No JS: in production the list and log are server-rendered, so the static frame still carries the message. The prototype fills the list with JS.

M–LEffort · app & auth

Interactive client component (search, keyboard, ARIA listbox), grid system, two new families. No motion library. Budget: Plex Sans 3 weights + Plex Mono 2, subset.

Auth: easiest of the three, as the auth pages are already UI. Logo: the wordmark must ship as SVG so Geist isn’t a third font; the prototype loads Geist 600/700 only for it.

Recommendation: B, Personal & warm, with C’s sync log on the product pages

B is the only direction that solves “nothing is ownable” while strengthening the calm, private tone. For switchers and teams, borrow C’s sync-log component for /features and /security, restyled as an index card. Keep A as the low-risk fallback: its token work (Geist, green scale, a warm neutral) is also step one of B, so it isn’t wasted either way.

Before choosing, test the three heroes on 5–8 people from the target audiences (a family organiser, a switcher, a small-team admin). This exploration has had no user research.

Shared notesContent, facts and build reality

Fact corrections vs P49 copy

  • Pro: “every sync source” → “up to 5 sync sources, 5 devices” (billing.ts).
  • Privacy fact: “no waiting period” → deletion after a 30-day grace period.
  • “Works on iPhone, Android and the web” → “no app to install”. Android sync isn’t in the verified fact list.
  • Comparison row “Syncs natively to iPhone and Android” → “Keeps Google, iCloud and Fastmail in step”.
  • Merge undo stated as 30 days.

Honesty constraints held

  • No testimonials, logos, user counts, ratings or uptime, and no placeholders for them.
  • No backup-encryption claim and no webhooks. Outlook is not mentioned.
  • Prices are Stripe slots; Family/Teams limits not in the brief are shown as billing.ts slots on /pricing.

Build reality

  • Same content model and component names in all three. Each direction is a token set plus a skin, which fits the --mkt-* plain-CSS setup.
  • All motion is CSS transitions on transform and opacity, triggered once. No library, no scroll-jacking.
  • Headers: signed-out, signed-in (review bar toggle or ?signed=1) and the mobile menu below 980px.
  • Docs and legal pages would inherit the header, footer and type scale only.
